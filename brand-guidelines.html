Brand guidelines · v1.0

Roy's Furniture Repair

Warm & Artisanal — a design system derived from the shop emblem. Wood, parchment, oak and rust; slab-serif headings on a humanist sans. Built to make a homeowner with a broken recliner feel confident enough to call.

Direction

The logo is a hand-crafted emblem — wood-grain lettering, crossed hammer and wrench, a parchment ribbon banner, deep espresso outlines. It reads artisan workshop, not corporate services firm.

Tactile, not slick

Trust and craftsmanship are the conversion levers. Warmth, texture and evidence of a real person beat polish and gradients.

Not another blue handyman site

The retired #3498db / #e74c3c palette clashed with the emblem and looked like every competitor in local search.

Restraint on texture

Wood grain and paper noise register as warmth, never as a background image. If you notice the texture, it is too strong.

Contrast & accessibility

Every pairing below was measured against WCAG 2.1: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text (≥24px, or ≥19px bold) and for meaningful non-text graphics. Twenty of twenty-two proposed pairings passed outright.

Two changes to the proposed system

  1. No hex value was repainted. The palette as specified is sound. Both problems were tokens being asked to do a job they cannot do, so the fix is scoping, not colour-picking — which keeps the palette faithful to the logo.
  2. --oak is a surface token, not a text token. Oak on cream measures 2.96:1 — it fails even the 3:1 non-text threshold. Use it for fills, icon backings and active indicators. For oak-toned text on light surfaces use --wood-warm, which is already in the palette and clears 4.5:1 on every light surface. Oak is safe as text on --wood-espresso (4.76:1).
  3. --star is dark-surface only. At 2.30:1 on cream it fails badly. It clears AA on espresso (6.13:1) and wood-dark (4.54:1). In practice this is moot on the live site: the star row uses the ⭐ emoji, which renders in its own colours and ignores CSS color entirely. The token is scoped so nobody later mistakes it for a safe text colour.

Non-negotiables

  • Every interactive element carries a visible :focus-visible ring — 3px --rust, 3px offset.
  • Colour never carries meaning alone: pair it with a glyph, label or position.
  • Tap targets are at least 44×44px.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: reduce disables every transition and animation.
  • Body text never sits below 16px.

Typography

Two families, four weights. Bitter is a slab serif that echoes the emblem's chunky woodblock lettering without imitating it. Source Sans 3 is a humanist sans, legible at small sizes and warm enough not to fight the slab. Both load from Google Fonts with display=swap and a full system fallback stack.

Rules

  • One h1 per page. Never skip a heading level for visual effect.
  • Bitter for headings only — it is not a body face at 16px.
  • Body measure caps at 68 characters.
  • Sentence case in UI labels; existing marketing copy stays exactly as written.
  • Never letterspace lowercase body text. Uppercase eyebrows take 0.16em.

Section rhythm

Alternate --parchment-soft and --cream so sections read as distinct bands. --parchment is reserved for callouts and the ribbon device — using it as a full band flattens the hierarchy.

Logo usage

The emblem is the whole identity — there is no separate wordmark or symbol lockup. The artwork must never be altered. Resize proportionally, pad, and composite onto a background. Nothing else.

Approved backgrounds

--cream · preferred

--parchment · print and packaging

--wood-espresso · site header, dark contexts

--oak · avoid — too little separation

Clear space

Keep clear space on all four sides equal to the height of the hammer head in the emblem — roughly 25% of the logo's total height. Nothing crosses this boundary: no text, no rules, no photo edges, no other logos.

Do and don't

Do

  • Scale proportionally with LANCZOS resampling
  • Preserve the alpha channel on every transparent export
  • Pad with a flat approved background colour
  • Place on cream, parchment or espresso
  • Export from the full-resolution original every time

Don't

  • Recolour, tint, or apply filters, gradients or drop shadows
  • Crop, rotate, skew, stretch, or redraw any part of it
  • Add an outline, glow, or container stroke
  • Place it on a busy photograph or a mid-tone oak fill
  • Rebuild the ribbon text as live type in another font
  • Upscale a small export — go back to the original

Motion

Motion confirms an action or reveals content. It never decorates.

InteractionDuration / easingBehaviour
Card hover250ms ease−4px translate, shadow deepens
Button hover200ms ease−2px translate, background darkens
Scroll reveal600ms ease-outFade in, +30px → 0, fired once by IntersectionObserver
Sticky nav300ms ease-outSlides down from −100%
Mobile nav sheet400ms cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)Expands, items stagger 50ms apart
Toasts300ms easeSlide in from the right, slide out on dismiss

Everything above collapses to 0.01ms under @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce). Content that fades in on scroll must be visible by default and animated from that state, so a reader who disables motion never sees a blank page.
